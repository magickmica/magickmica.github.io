The Midnight Pavilion♥ 5↻ 0

The Midnight Pavilion Everyone in the village believed the pavilion had been built for dances. By day, it sat quietly in the center of the Moonflower Garden, wrapped in climbing roses and silver vines. White lilies surrounded its marble columns, and crystal lanterns hung from the ceiling like frozen stars. Visitors came to admire the flowers. Poets came to write. Lovers came to make promises. No one noticed the clock. It was hidden beneath the pavilion floor, buried deep below the roots of the moonflowers. A clock older than history. A clock that did not count hours. It counted destinations. Every night at exactly midnight, the pavilion awakened. The lanterns filled with violet light. The roses closed. The moonflowers opened. And the marble floor began to hum. Those standing beneath the pavilion at the stroke of midnight would vanish. Not disappear. Travel. The strange thing was that the pavilion never asked where you wanted to go. It decided for you. And for reasons no one understood, its first destination was almost always Ancient Egypt. Travelers would step beneath the pavilion surrounded by roses and emerge moments later beneath a blazing desert sun. Golden sand stretched endlessly toward the horizon. Great temples shimmered in the distance. The Nile sparkled like liquid emerald beneath the stars. Some found themselves walking through bustling markets filled with perfumes and lapis jewelry. Others stood among colossal statues whose eyes seemed older than time itself. A few lucky travelers witnessed the pyramids while they were still new. No one knew why the pavilion favored Egypt. Some believed an ancient priestess had built it. Others claimed the pavilion itself had first been created there thousands of years ago and simply liked returning home. The most curious theory belonged to Alice. Alice had discovered the pavilion accidentally. One moonlit evening she wandered across a small bridge that crossed a silver stream winding through the midnight garden. Moonflowers glowed along the water's edge. Fireflies floated between the roses. The air smelled faintly of jasmine and secrets. At the center of the garden stood the pavilion. Its crystal lanterns were already glowing. Alice checked her pocket watch. 11:59. "That seems suspicious," she whispered. The clock struck midnight. The lanterns burst into brilliant starlight. The marble floor beneath her feet transformed into a swirling galaxy. Then the entire pavilion vanished. A moment later Alice found herself standing beneath a sky unlike any she had ever seen. The Milky Way stretched across the heavens. Massive pyramids towered before her. Golden torches flickered along stone pathways. And somewhere beyond the dunes, music echoed through the desert night. Ancient Egypt. Again. The pavilion seemed very proud of itself. "Oh," Alice sighed. "Not Egypt again." The wind answered by scattering glowing desert flowers across the sand. Alice laughed. Apparently even magical time-travel pavilions had favorite destinations. Still, she stayed. Because everyone knew the secret. If you survived Egypt until the next midnight... The pavilion would open again. And then it could take you absolutely anywhere. Victorian London. Atlantis. A crystal city on the moon. A forgotten library hidden between centuries. The last garden at the end of time. Or a place no map had ever named. The pavilion never explained its choices. It simply waited beneath the moonflowers. Patient. Silent. Counting destinations. And every midnight, somewhere in the garden, another curious traveler stepped beneath its glowing roof and wondered where they might wake up next.
