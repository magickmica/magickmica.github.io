Good Girls Go To Heaven…

we go to another galaxy

The hallway isn't made of paint. Every inch pulses with living colour. The floor reflects futures that haven't happened yet; the ceiling swirls like liquid galaxies poured into a kaleidoscope. Walking through it feels like floating inside a cosmic lava lamp while the universe quietly whispers your real name.

As you move deeper, gravity begins forgetting its job. The glitter drifts sideways. Time folds itself into origami. Old memories dissolve into stardust while impossible new ones quietly appear.

A planet where fairies harvest comets like flowers.
An ocean made entirely of melted rainbows.
An endless roller rink where UFOs skate beneath disco moons.
A library where every book tells the story of a different version of you.

The farther you travel, the brighter your imagination becomes. Eventually your thoughts begin creating the scenery around you. Think of candy-coloured galaxies — they appear. Imagine crystal forests — they bloom beside you. Picture impossible stars, and the ceiling invents new constellations astronomers haven't discovered yet.

Some travellers never return. Not because they're lost, but because they finally remembered where they came from.

The hallway never asks if you're ready.
It simply sparkles… and opens.

Part II — The Galaxy That Dreamed First

The moment she stepped through the final doorway, the universe blinked. Not exploded. Not expanded. It simply remembered her.

The galaxy beyond wasn't floating in space. Space was floating inside it. There were no suns — the light came from flowers. Millions of cosmic blossoms opened and closed with every heartbeat of the universe, releasing clouds of fairy dust that became butterflies halfway through the breeze.

Nothing here had shadows. Every living thing glowed from within. And then she noticed them: the Door Garden. Millions of glowing doors stretching farther than imagination itself — moonstone, candy-coloured crystal, stained-glass galaxies. One opened into a dream someone would have tomorrow. One led to the last sunset of a forgotten civilisation. One simply contained another hallway, glittering, waiting, endless.

An elderly fairy floated toward her wearing wings stitched from fragments of the Milky Way. “You finally made it.” — “I've never been here before.” — The fairy laughed. “Oh, yes you have.”

She waved her hand, and every sparkle became a tiny window. Inside each one, a different version of her.

One painting galaxies.
One flying beside whales through deep space.
One dancing with aliens beneath a disco moon.
One building castles from comets.
One riding a dragon woven from northern lights.
One walking through the hallway for the very first time… again.

“This galaxy isn't where dreams come after you imagine them.
It's where dreams are born before they ever find you.”

The Object Is More

what a notorious graffiti writer taught me about leaving a mark

There are artists who chase perfection. And then there are artists who chase presence. CAP — the infamous New York graffiti writer — wasn't trying to paint the prettiest letters in the city. His mission was simple: be impossible to ignore.

To CAP, graffiti wasn't a painting. It was proof of existence. Every tag whispered “I was here.” Every wall became a page in an endless conversation between strangers. Every train became a moving constellation of human persistence.

I was hereI was hereI was hereI was here I was hereI was hereI was hereI was here I was hereI was hereI was hereI was here

We live in a world obsessed with perfect portfolios, flawless grids, curated aesthetics and algorithms that reward polish over personality. But somewhere beneath all that, there's still something beautifully primitive about simply leaving a mark. Not because it's perfect. Because it's real.

How many people spend their lives waiting until they're “good enough” before creating anything? How many incredible ideas never escape someone's notebook? How many dreams quietly evaporate while someone tweaks the colours, rewrites the paragraph, waits for the stars to align? Meanwhile, someone else simply starts. They post. They paint. They write. Again. And again. And again.

The universe itself doesn't seem interested in perfection. It prefers repetition.

Stars pulse
Planets orbit
Waves return
Trees grow rings
Galaxies spiral

Reality leaves its signature everywhere.
Maybe we're meant to do the same.

Maybe that's why I've always loved weathered posters peeling off brick walls. Old magazines with coffee stains. Misprinted books. VHS tapes that still play. Data-moshed pixels. Half-erased signs. Cracked paint. Artifacts carrying the fingerprints of time. They aren't perfect. They're alive. They've been somewhere. They've survived something. The object became more than the object.

Perhaps the greatest work of art isn't the masterpiece hanging in a museum. Perhaps it's the accumulation of thousands of small acts of creation scattered throughout a lifetime. Tiny echoes. Tiny portals. Tiny reminders that someone dared to imagine differently.

Leave the mark.
Write the story.
Build the impossible world.
Make the strange thing.
Publish the weird idea.
Plant the seed.

Not because it's flawless. Because someday someone will stumble across it and realise they weren't the only one dreaming this way. ✨
