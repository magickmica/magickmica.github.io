Forest Service Opens Its Bigfoot Files

Three records quietly posted to the government’s reading room describe giant footprints in Oregon and Montana, and one 1982 investigation that ended in suspicion of a hoax

Umatilla National Forest · June 1982

Biologist Finds Tracks Too Shallow for a 300-Pound Giant

Team’s own boots sank deeper than the mystery prints; Border Patrol man later calls them a likely hoax

From the files of the U.S. Forest Service

Mill Creek Watershed, near Pendleton, Ore. — Reports of enormous footprints in the watershed brought a Forest Service team into the woods on June 17, 1982. They visited two sites along Low Creek, the first about a mile and a half above Mill Creek, and took measurements, color photographs and plaster casts.

Wildlife biologist Rodney L. Johnson set down the findings in a memo the next day, June 18. The prints were large, but their depth bothered him. The team’s boots sank farther into the mud than the prints did, a poor fit for an animal said to weigh hundreds of pounds.

The gait raised more questions. The stride held at about four feet on gentle and steep ground alike, the prints showed no slipping on the slopes, and some of the debris around them appeared to have been cleared away. Johnson wrote that the impressions showed signs of something being rocked back and forth to make them.

His conclusion: the tracks were made by something other than a two-footed creature of 300 pounds or more.

A second memo followed on July 28, 1982, from Joel Hardin of the U.S. Border Patrol, a noted tracker. Hardin judged the prints a likely hoax made with an artificial foot.

The file, now public, includes the two memos and the team’s photographs of the prints, laid out with markers in the manner of a crime scene.

Petty Creek, Montana · 1940s

Hunters Trail Tracks That Carried Off an Elk

A first-hand account set down around 1980

Account of Leo T. Kinney

In a short typed document written around 1980, Leo T. Kinney looked back on a winter in the 1940s near Petty Creek, west of Missoula, when he and his companions found huge two-legged tracks in the snow.

The men followed them for about half a mile. By Kinney’s telling, whatever made them had lifted an elk whole, clear off the ground, and carried it. Where the trail crossed a fallen log about three feet thick, they could see the elk’s antlers had dragged across the snow and over the timber.

The account is the oldest of the three in the release, and the only one with no investigation attached.

Plains, Sanders County, Montana · January 2003

Boy, 8, Finds Hundreds of Giant Prints in Snow by Sawmill

Neighbor tracks the trail for two hours before it vanishes on rocky ground

From a clipping of the Valley Press / Mineral Independent

River Road West — Eight-year-old Austin Petersen was visiting the home of Jim and Gladys Rohweder, next door to his father Dean’s place, when he found the prints in fresh snow beside the couple’s shop and sawmill. There were hundreds of them, clearest out in the open yard.

Each print ran about 16½ inches long and 8½ inches across just behind the toes, narrowing to a heel about four inches wide. They showed toes and an arch, and the neighbors said they looked more human than animal. The average stride was about five feet.

The trail came from the direction of Eddy Creek, crossed the yard, turned and headed for the Clark Fork River. Neighbor Bob Mesing, a contractor, followed it for about two hours until it faded in the woods some 100 yards off, where the ground turned hard and rocky.

“What else could it be.”Bob Mesing, River Road West

The night before, Rohweder’s motion-sensor light had switched on at the shop, and Dean Petersen woke to his dog barking but saw nothing. A large pile of dung lay near the tracks. Judging by the size of the prints, Petersen put the animal at 500 to 1,000 pounds.

Mesing said he had started out a skeptic. Gladys Rohweder said she once saw a hairy, two-legged creature soon after the couple moved to the road some 30 years earlier. Rain washed most of the prints away the next day.

What the Files Do Not Say

None of the three records proves the creature exists. The 1982 file is the only one with a formal investigation, and its two investigators both doubted the tracks were real.

The 2003 dung was never sent to a lab, and no one called the authorities about the prints.
