Alice Visits Willy Wonky's
Carnival Candy Factory

you are the magic

Not ordinary clouds — cotton candy weather systems, floating gently beneath a ceiling that had somehow forgotten gravity. Glass tubes carried shimmering sugar through transparent pipes like glowing rivers of liquid starlight, and giant crystalline machines spun impossible flavours into existence while tiny rainbow sparks escaped from the brass valves.

At the centre stood Willy Wonky, in a velvet coat stitched entirely from carnival tickets that changed colour whenever nobody was looking. “You're just in time for today's Cotton Candy Consciousness Trials.”

DREAM DENSITYNOSTALGIA CONCENTRATETUESDAYUNFINISHED WISHES

He handed her a tiny silver spoon holding what looked like an ordinary pink cloud. Flavour Number 743. She took one bite, and the laboratory dissolved. For three seconds she became a carousel horse dreaming it was a constellation. Then she was seven years old. Then ninety-seven. Then a teacup. Then somehow all of those things at once.

When the room came back, her shoes had turned transparent. “Excellent,” said Wonky, scribbling furiously. “Only minor temporal frosting.”

“Nothing appeared to follow scientific principles. Everything appeared to follow fairy tale physics.”

Bluetasted like forgotten songs.
Lavenderdissolved into tiny butterflies that politely apologised before disappearing.
Goldmade shadows giggle.
Greenconvinced houseplants to gossip.
One empty jarPossibility.

Wonky pulled a glittering lever and the machine inhaled every colour in the room. For one magnificent moment the cotton candy became the sky, the sky became the carnival, the carnival became a story — and the story quietly wrapped itself around Alice like the sweetest scarf imaginable.

Afterwards everything looked perfectly normal. Except the clouds now smelled faintly of strawberries, gravity seemed optional, and somewhere in Alice's pocket was a tiny puff of cotton candy that glowed whenever someone imagined impossible things. She never ate it.

“Some candies are too magical to disappear. Some are meant to remind us that reality has always been under construction.”

Cosmic Kitchen Magazine

the august issue, served warm

There are foods that fill your stomach, and foods that fill your spirit. Long before recipes were measured in teaspoons and timers, meals were guided by intuition, seasons, moonlight and ritual. A loaf of bread carried the warmth of the sun. A cup of tea held the memory of rain.

In a world of notifications and endless scrolling, comfort food becomes an act of rebellion. It says: sit, stay, taste this moment.

“Sometimes the most extraordinary thing isn't travelling to another galaxy. It's discovering one in your morning coffee.”

Why cosmic brunch matters. The secret isn't the ingredients, it's the atmosphere. Open a window. Let sunlight pour across the table. Put on music that feels like a forgotten summer. Use the special dishes you've been saving. Light a candle even if it's noon.

The universe is full of spectacular events — eclipses, meteor showers, comets crossing the sky. But brunch reminds us that wonder doesn't always arrive dramatically. Sometimes it arrives disguised as pancakes.
